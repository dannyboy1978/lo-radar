If every marketing idea in your calendar depends on a rate drop, 2026 has been a hard year. The 30-year fixed has been running around 7 percent for most of it. Refi applications are down 60 percent year over year. The rate-drop playbook is empty.

The 27 ideas below are the ones that work in a flat or rising rate market. They are built around equity, ARM resets, HELOC headroom, service-first touches, borrower life-stage events, and the referral network that past clients already hold. Every one is RESPA-safe and compliance-aware. Pick five.

## The ARM reset plays

**1. The 12-month heads-up.** Every 5/1, 7/1, and 10/1 ARM in your book has a known reset date. At T minus 12, nobody else is calling. A single low-pressure note ("your 7/1 ARM resets in July 2027, I will be in touch at T minus 6 with options") makes you the LO who is on top of this. See the [ARM Reset Playbook](/blog/arm-reset-calendar-twelve-months-out/) for the full cadence.

**2. The ARM reset calculator page.** A borrower-facing calculator at your own URL that shows the reset math (current rate, today's index, worst-case cap, fixed-rate alternative). Capture consent as the borrower runs their own numbers. Also an SEO page that ranks for "arm adjustment calculator" and "7/1 arm reset."

**3. The ARM-era specific series.** Borrowers who took 7/1 ARMs in 2019 at ~3.8 percent are facing a very specific decision in 2026. A seven-email micro-series walking that cohort through let-it-ride vs recast vs refi vs sell converts better than a generic newsletter.

## The equity and HELOC plays

**4. The equity milestone touch.** When a borrower's equity crosses $100K, $200K, $500K, send a plain-English note. The milestone is the reason to talk; the HELOC or cash-out conversation follows.

**5. The HELOC vs cash-out blended-rate analysis.** For locked-in sub-5-percent first mortgages, running HELOC vs cash-out vs closed-end second as a blended effective rate is the most valuable analysis a loan officer can offer in this market. Three paths, one borrower, one honest recommendation.

**6. The PMI cancellation call.** Conventional borrowers whose LTV has crossed 80 percent can request PMI cancellation. Most borrowers do not know. A "you may be eligible to cancel $X a month of PMI" call is a service-first touch that costs you nothing and often earns a call back.

**7. The FHA MIP drop analysis.** FHA borrowers whose appreciation makes a conventional refi worth it (even at a higher rate) to drop MIP permanently. The all-in payment comparison is often surprising.

## The anniversary and lifecycle plays

**8. The year-one check-in.** First-year borrowers get the simplest call: "how is the house, anything working better or worse than you expected, any questions on the first tax return." Zero sell. Builds the referral baseline.

**9. The year-three equity-and-plans call.** Year three is the first realistic refi-or-HELOC window and the first meaningful equity position for most borrowers. A 15-minute call to walk through current numbers and what the next three years could look like.

**10. The year-five retention conversation.** Year five is the highest-conversion refi year across almost every book. The conversation is explicit: here is where you stand, here are three possible moves, here is what I would do.

**11. The year-seven and year-ten consolidation window.** By year seven, most borrowers have other debt (card, auto, student). A rate/term refi is usually dead, but a cash-out for debt consolidation can genuinely improve the household's math. Run the real numbers.

**12. The college-funding conversation.** Year 10 to 15 of a loan is often the year the oldest child enters high school. The conversation shifts from "refinance" to "tap equity for tuition without touching your first mortgage." HELOC-first thinking.

## The life-stage plays (from data you already hold)

**13. The 1003 life-event touch.** The original 1003 application holds dependents' ages, household income, and employment. Project forward: a borrower whose youngest leaves for college in 2027 has a predictable downsize window. Call them in 2026 about what the move would look like.

**14. The HECM eligibility note for 62+ borrowers.** Past borrowers turning 62 with meaningful equity are candidates for a HECM credit line or a reverse. Most LOs skip the conversation. The ones who have it earn an outsized share of the business they open.

**15. The homeowners insurance renewal letter.** Homeowners insurance premiums are up sharply in many markets in 2026. A short note alongside the renewal cycle offering to review the full payment picture is a service-first touch that opens conversations about escrow, refinancing, or a HELOC.

**16. The property-tax protest reminder.** In Texas, Arizona, California, and other states with protest windows, a January reminder that your borrower's property tax might be worth protesting is service-first and often appreciated. If they win a reduction, their payment math changes and a conversation follows.

## The service-first and relationship plays

**17. The disaster-ZIP check-in.** When FEMA declares a disaster, every past borrower in the affected ZIP codes gets a check-in: forbearance options, insurance claims process, rebuild financing path. Being the LO who called after the fire earns loyalty that rate alerts never will.

**18. The closing-day photo book.** A printed photo book of the closing day photos, mailed on the one-year anniversary. Costs you $15. Shows up as the most-remembered touch for most borrowers who get one.

**19. The post-close CPA handoff.** First year after close, offer to send the borrower's mortgage interest and property tax figures directly to their CPA (with the borrower's permission) ahead of tax prep. Service, not referral; the CPA remembers who sent it.

**20. The move-in care package.** For a first-time buyer close, a small care package of essentials (coffee, local snacks, a utility-setup checklist). The Yelp-review equivalent in the mortgage world.

## The content and visibility plays

**21. The LO's own weekly rate recap, in a 2-minute video.** Not the vendor-templated one. The LO on their own phone, 90 seconds, on the Monday rate action and what it means for a past client's refi or HELOC math. Post on LinkedIn, text to the book.

**22. The "ask me anything" quarterly office hour.** A borrower-facing Zoom, 45 minutes, LO answers whatever past clients bring. Low friction, high trust, a modest number of borrowers show up, every one of them becomes a referral source.

**23. The neighborhood-specific market update.** A quarterly one-page note on a borrower's specific zip code or neighborhood: median sales, inventory, days on market, average sale-to-list. Hyper-local, high relevance, zero-sell.

**24. The LinkedIn story cadence.** Three posts a week, one about a borrower outcome (with permission), one about a market observation, one personal. Consistent over 12 months, this is how a loan officer builds an inbound pipeline.

## The ecosystem plays

**25. The agent co-marketing calculator.** A refinance-break-even or HELOC-vs-cash-out calculator, co-branded with a referral agent, with each party paying their proportional share of the ad spend (per [RESPA Section 8](/compliance/respa-section-8-past-client-marketing)). Agents love tools they can share; buyers love calculators they can run.

**26. The referral rewards for past clients (RESPA-safe).** A small thank-you (gift card to a local restaurant, $25 to $50) to past borrowers who refer a buyer who closes. Section 8's bar on referral compensation applies to settlement-service providers; a past borrower is a consumer, not a settlement-service provider. Check with your compliance officer on your specific state rules; most are fine.

**27. The past-client advisory board.** Three to five past borrowers, once a year, over coffee or dinner, asked what the LO could do better and what they wish other buyers knew. Pure relationship investment. Highest-retention loan officers in the country do a version of this.

## Pick five and ship them before January

The LOs who end 2026 ahead of where they started it are the ones who pick a handful of these and run them with discipline, not the ones who try all 27 and ship none.
