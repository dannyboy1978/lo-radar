The Government Accountability Office published [GAO-27-108874](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108874) on November 7, 2026, the first federal review of how the Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act (HPPA) actually landed in the eight months since it took effect on March 4, 2026. For any loan officer running a book right now, three findings matter and should shape the next twelve months of pipeline planning.

## Finding 1: consumer complaints about unsolicited mortgage calls fell sharply

The GAO measured complaints to the CFPB, the FCC, and the state attorneys general from March 4, 2026 through October 31, 2026 and compared the volume against the equivalent pre-HPPA baseline. Complaint volume on unsolicited mortgage solicitations dropped dramatically. Enforcement actions in the eight-month window were correspondingly rare.

The practical implication: HPPA is working on the thing it was designed to work on. A consumer who sits down for a credit-pull in 2026 does not get the pre-HPPA phone-call-and-text flood. For a loan officer pitching past clients today, this has a second-order effect: a past client getting your call stands out, because they are no longer getting ten competing ones.

## Finding 2: independent mortgage brokers saw lead-cost shifts in both directions

The GAO's survey work showed that independent mortgage brokers who had relied on purchased trigger leads reported pipeline shortfalls through roughly the first four months after HPPA took effect. By the eight-month mark, the brokers who had rebuilt their pipeline around past-client monitoring, borrower-initiated opt-ins, and strengthened real-estate-agent referral networks were reporting lead-cost-per-closed-loan at or below the pre-HPPA baseline.

The brokers who had not rebuilt reported worse per-loan economics than before HPPA. A small share had exited the business.

Two lessons for an IMB or broker shop:

1. Past-client monitoring is not a nice-to-have anymore. It is the pipeline. Shops that invested in a book-intelligence workflow in 2026 are already running at or below pre-HPPA lead cost; shops that have not are running above.
2. Borrower-initiated opt-ins (consent capture on the LO's own site) are the cleanest post-HPPA intent signal, and the brokers who built them earliest are running the strongest pipelines.

## Finding 3: existing-relationship holders captured a larger share of refinance activity

This is the finding that matters most for a bank or an IMB with a servicing-retained portfolio. Depository institutions, servicers, and originators with HPPA-standing access ran a higher recapture rate in the post-HPPA period than in the pre-HPPA baseline. The GAO's measurement is coarse (it does not identify individual lenders), but the direction is unambiguous.

In context: the industry-baseline recapture rate sits around [14 percent](/recapture-rate), with top-quartile shops at 30 and best-in-class approaching 40. The GAO's finding is that the post-HPPA structural shift is widening the gap between the shops with existing-relationship standing and everyone else. A bank home-lending head looking at this report has an easier internal case for a past-client intelligence investment than they did in 2024.

## What the report does not say

The GAO's report is a first look. It does not quantify:

- The rate at which specific independent brokers exited the business. Estimates in the industry press range widely.
- The impact on consumer refinance activity volume (did consumers refinance less because they were called less, or did the same consumers refinance with different lenders?).
- The enforcement posture for 2027. The report notes that the CFPB is still finalizing the examination manual for HPPA-related conduct.

The GAO plans a follow-up review in late 2027 after the first full year of enforcement experience.

## What the report means for an LO reading it today

Three operating conclusions:

- **The window to build a past-client workflow at a cost advantage is still open.** The GAO's brokers-who-rebuilt data makes clear that the shops moving now are capturing the structural tailwind. The ones who wait are competing against them.
- **Borrower-initiated opt-ins are higher-leverage than they were twelve months ago.** Any landing page that captures "text me when I can save $300 a month" is building a consent asset that competing lenders no longer have a shortcut to.
- **If you have servicing access, use it.** The recapture-rate gap the GAO documented is going to compound. A bank LO or any IMB with a serviced book has an unusually clean 2027 runway.
