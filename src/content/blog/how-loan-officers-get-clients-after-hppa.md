Through 2024 and most of 2025, the honest answer to "how does a loan officer get clients" for a lot of independents was "buy trigger leads and work the phones." That answer stopped working on March 4, 2026, when the Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act took effect and closed third-party purchase of mortgage trigger leads for independent lenders.

Eight months later, with the GAO's first review of HPPA out ([GAO-27-108874](/blog/gao-report-hppa-trigger-leads-2026/)), the picture of what replaced the pre-HPPA pipeline is clearer. The loan officers running strong books in late 2026 are running a specific playbook. This is it.

## The four legal sources that replaced trigger leads

Every post-HPPA LO pipeline is built out of some combination of these four. The percentages below are indicative, based on the mix the strongest shops are reporting in late 2026.

1. **Past-client book monitoring.** ~40 to 60 percent of total closed volume for an established LO.
2. **Borrower-initiated opt-ins** (consent capture on the LO's own site). ~10 to 20 percent and rising.
3. **Real-estate-agent referral** under RESPA-safe arrangements. ~20 to 40 percent of purchase volume.
4. **Servicing / existing-relationship portfolio** (for bank LOs and IMBs with retained servicing). Variable; typically ~15 to 35 percent for lenders with one.

The pre-HPPA category that is gone: third-party trigger leads. Independent brokers who have not replaced it with the four above have smaller pipelines now than in 2024. See [trigger lead alternatives](/blog/trigger-lead-alternatives-for-loan-officers/) for the longer list of ways to fill the gap.

## The weekly rhythm that works

The loan officers hitting their numbers in this market run a specific weekly cadence. Not a quarterly campaign, not a monthly cycle. Weekly. The rhythm:

**Monday morning: the briefing.** Open the past-client book, review the top three to five opportunities for the week ranked by expected value. These are the calls that will produce closes in 30 to 90 days. A tool like [LO Radar](/) produces this list automatically; the LOs who do it manually from a servicing report typically see a quarter of the volume.

**Monday through Wednesday: the calls.** Book-first. The reason it is Monday-through-Wednesday: client answer rates are measurably higher Monday through Wednesday than Thursday or Friday, and the LO's own energy for difficult conversations peaks early in the week. Five to eight substantive calls over three mornings beats fifteen quick dials over five.

**Wednesday afternoon: the agent touches.** Co-marketing partners, past referring agents, agents in the top three zip codes of the LO's book. Short notes, market observations, property updates. Not sales pitches.

**Thursday: the content and the opt-in capture.** Record the weekly rate recap (90 seconds on a phone, post to LinkedIn). Update the LO's own site if there are new scenarios worth capturing. Any opt-in forms (refi calculator, HELOC calculator, ARM reset estimator) that fired consent captures in the last week get a response.

**Friday: the loss review and the next-week plan.** Which loans did not close this week? Why? What was the competing lender's edge? Which of the top three or five calls planned Monday actually happened, and what is next week's set? LO Radar's attribution dashboard surfaces the loss data automatically; LOs working from a manual tracker usually skip this step and compound the problem.

**2. The consent record this produces is cleaner than anything on the pre-HPPA menu, and it is a one-time build that produces leads for years.

**3. A written RESPA-safe co-marketing template.** One agent partner, one monthly ad spend, each party paying their proportional share, documented at fair market value. The RESPA posture is strict; see [RESPA Section 8](/compliance/respa-section-8-past-client-marketing) for the fact patterns that hold up. Once the template is in place, adding the second and third agent partner is a copy-paste.

## The four tactical plays that compound over months

Beyond the three investments, four specific plays produce disproportionate pipeline for the LOs who run them consistently.

**Twelve-month ARM lead time.** Every 5/1, 7/1, 10/1 ARM in the book has a known reset date. Starting the conversation at T minus 12 instead of T minus 3 produces a 3x conversion improvement because nobody else is calling the borrower yet. See the [ARM Reset Playbook](/blog/arm-reset-calendar-twelve-months-out/).

**Permit records.** A building permit pulled on a past client's property is a HELOC or cash-out conversation before the contractor's financing partner has it. Public record. Free. LO Radar's Permit Radar runs this against the book daily for the counties it covers (Austin today).

**The weekly LinkedIn recap.** Three times a week for 12 months is what builds an inbound pipeline from LinkedIn. Not every post has to perform; consistency is the asset.

**The quarterly client advisory group.** Three to five past borrowers, over coffee or on a Zoom, asked what you could do better and what other buyers should know. Pure relationship investment. Highest-retention LOs in the country run a version of this.

## What to stop doing

Three specific tactics that were viable in 2024 and are not in 2026:

- **Buying third-party mortgage trigger leads.** Not an option for independents post-HPPA. See [Are mortgage trigger leads illegal?](/blog/are-mortgage-trigger-leads-illegal/).
- **Mass-cold-SMS past clients without consent records.** The TCPA posture has sharpened and the state mini-TCPA overlays (Florida, Oklahoma, Maryland, Washington) are being enforced. See [TCPA for loan officers](/compliance/tcpa-for-loan-officers).
- **One-sided co-marketing arrangements.** Section 8 fact patterns that were loosely tolerated five years ago are enforcement targets now. Each party pays their proportional share; the one-sided arrangements are over.

## The honest summary

Getting clients as a loan officer in 2026 is a quieter game than it was in 2024. The 30-call-a-day trigger-lead workflow is gone. What replaced it is a disciplined weekly rhythm on a past-client book, a few compounding content and partnership investments, and software that does the book-ranking math so the LO can spend the week on the conversations rather than on the spreadsheet.

The LOs who built around this in 2026 are closing. The ones still waiting for 2023 to come back are not.
