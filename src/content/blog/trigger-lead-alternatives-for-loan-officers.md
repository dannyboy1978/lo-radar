The pre-HPPA mortgage trigger lead market, where any lender could buy a prescreened consumer report within 24 hours of a competing lender's credit pull, is closed. HPPA (effective March 4, 2026) restricts purchase of mortgage trigger leads to originators, servicers, and existing-relationship depositories. Independent lenders and brokers without one of those relationships cannot buy them at all.

For the LOs and shops who have spent the last 18 months rebuilding their pipeline, here are the seven lawful intent sources that are working in late 2026, ordered by signal strength.

## 1. Your own past-client book (the strongest single source)

This is the pipeline. Every loan you originated is a borrower you already have an FCRA permissible-purpose relationship with. Seven trigger categories are actionable from data you already hold: rate-drop opportunities, ARM resets inside the 12-month window, anniversary touches (year 1, 3, 5, 7, 10), HELOC headroom, cash-out opportunities, second-home signals, and credit-improvement events.

The gating factor is not the data; it is the workflow. A monthly manual scan of the servicing report finds maybe a quarter of the opportunities. A daily automated workflow with ranking, drafting, and compliance posture inline (which is what [LO Radar](/) does) finds all of them.

## 2. Borrower-initiated opt-ins on your own site

A past borrower (or any homeowner) who opts in to "text me when I can save $300 a month" or "when my equity passes $150,000" gives you the strongest possible consent record: timestamped, one-to-one, in the borrower's own words. The conversion rate on a borrower-set trigger dwarfs anything from a vendor-set alert, and the TCPA posture is the cleanest in the product.

Three pieces to build: a calculator or scenario tool on your site (refi break-even, HELOC vs cash-out, ARM reset), a consent form that captures the borrower's specific trigger condition, and a workflow that fires when the trigger hits.

## 3. The lender's or servicer's portfolio where you have standing

If you originate for a bank or IMB that services its own loans, you have access to the full servicing signal set under the existing-relationship exception: payoff quotes viewed in the portal, autopay cancellations, escrow changes, address updates, extra-principal patterns stopping. These are the strongest leading indicators of a payoff, often three to six weeks before the payoff request arrives. They are the single highest-leverage source for a bank LO, and they are the gap enterprise retention tools typically leave open at the loan officer level.

## 4. Real estate agent referral networks (structured correctly)

Real-estate-agent referral remains the largest single source of new purchase business for most loan officers. The RESPA Section 8 rules are strict: co-marketing requires each party to pay their proportional share of the actual promotional cost, and MSAs require documented fair-market services. See our [RESPA Section 8 guide](/compliance/respa-section-8-past-client-marketing) for the fact patterns that hold up and the ones that do not.

The structural shift post-HPPA: agents are also losing their old lead flows (listing-shopping behavioral data is under similar privacy pressure), which is making them more receptive to loan-officer-led co-marketing programs that actually produce buyers.

## 5. Building permit records (the under-used public-record source)

A building permit pulled on a past client's property is the strongest renovation-financing signal available and it is public record. A pool, solar, battery, EV charger, ADU, addition, or remodel permit is a HELOC or cash-out conversation before the contractor's financing partner has it. Match permits to the book daily; LO Radar's Permit Radar covers Austin today with Frisco, NYC, and Miami-Dade next in the pipeline.

No credit-bureau data, no FCRA exposure, HPPA-irrelevant. The gating factor is the ingestion pipeline (county-by-county data layer), which is expensive to build once and free to query forever.

## 6. VA IRRRL and FHA Streamline readiness monitoring

VA and FHA borrowers have well-defined streamline eligibility triggers: VA IRRRL requires 210 days from first payment, six payments on time, a 0.50-point rate reduction, and net-benefit math; FHA Streamline requires 210 days from closing, six payments, and a passing NTB test. Every VA or FHA loan in the book has a known date when eligibility opens. Monitoring that date and firing a borrower-friendly check-in at the exact right moment is one of the highest-retention plays available to any LO.

## 7. Escrow shock and property-tax reassessment

Homeowners insurance and property-tax reassessments are raising escrow payments faster than rates are moving in many markets in 2026. Pulling county assessment changes and estimating escrow increases per borrower produces a service-first outreach reason that is easy to defend under RESPA ("your payment may rise $280 a month next year; here are three options"). It is a reason to talk when rates are flat and recapture competitors are quiet.

## What is NOT on the list, and why

- **Buying trigger leads.** The third-party market is closed for independents. HPPA, March 4, 2026. See [Are mortgage trigger leads illegal?](/blog/are-mortgage-trigger-leads-illegal/) for the long version.
- **Credit inquiry scraping.** Any attempt to replicate the pre-HPPA trigger-lead stream through credit-adjacent data runs the same FCRA risk the ban was designed to close. Several vendors have tried; none have cleared the legal line.
- **Paid-search bidding on competitor names.** Still legal; still expensive; still a loss leader for most LOs. Lower on this list than any first-party source because the intent quality is lower than opt-in and the per-lead cost is higher than past-client monitoring.

## The playbook that holds up

Build around 1, 2, and 3 first. They are the three sources with the strongest consent basis, the lowest ongoing cost, and the highest conversion. Layer 4 (RESPA-safe agent co-marketing) and 5 (permit records) once the base is running. Keep 6 and 7 as cadence enhancers that surface reasons to call when the rate market is quiet.
